FCC-ee QRL Cryogenic Flow Model

At CERN, I work on cryogenic studies for the FCC project. As part of this work, I created a Python tool that models the helium mass flow and pressure distribution along the QRL (cryogenic distribution line). The QRL feeds helium to dozens of superconducting cryomodules along the tunnel.

Cryomodule Bi-Phase Line Sizing

Another project of mine at CERN involves sizing the bi-phase lines for the FCC-ee superconducting radiofrequency cryomodules. The bi-phase line is the horizontal tube above the cavities that contains saturated liquid helium and its vapour. It must be large enough to keep the vapour speed low while holding enough liquid to handle operational failures.

I made a Python tool that calculates the liquid-level profile along the bi-phase tube and the time it takes for the line to dry out in the event of an interruption in the helium supply.

Maximum Credible Incident (MCI) Analysis

For the FCC-ee superconducting RF cryomodules at CERN, I investigated the pressure differential during a Maximum Credible Incident (MCI) event.

An MCI event causes an extreme heat flux into the helium bath. The resulting boil-off must be vented safely through the inner piping without over-pressurizing the cryomodule. I calculated the venting mass flow and compared design changes that would limit the pressure differential within the cryomodules during such an event.

Hydrogen Motorcycle

The project aimed to establish the feasibility of powering a motorcycle with a hydrogen fuel cell. The goals were to source components, calculate the total cost of the powertrain design, and perform high-level component integration and simulation. My contributions included creating CAD components, designing the fuel-cell schematics, and conducting Simulink simulations to assess the performance of the fuel cell, supercapacitor, DC/DC converter, and motor system under various loading conditions.

Arduino Memory Game

The Arduino Joystick Memory Game is an interactive project of mine where players replicate sequences of blinking lights using a joystick. The game setup includes four LEDs arranged in a diamond pattern on a breadboard, with sequences of blinks generated randomly. As players successfully mimic these patterns, the sequences become increasingly complex, adding extra blinks with each level.

Suspension Bushing Test Fixture

At Lucid Motors, one of my projects involved designing a bushing test fixture. The final design was deemed free of proprietary material and can be seen on the right.

The fixture was composed of multiple CNC-machined steel components that clamped the bushings and attached them to a CTW LA-48 actuator. The design process was iterative and involved many revisions. FEA was used to improve the design by reducing material, limiting deflection, and ensuring that the parts could handle maximum loading conditions.

The actuator was programmed using chirp signals to test the bushings under dynamic and static conditions. I then created a MATLAB program to analyse data from various suppliers to ensure that they met performance benchmarks.

EcoCar Sideview Mirrors

Another EcoCar project of mine involved designing an aerodynamic side-view mirror. The design included tailored draft angles that could be integrated into the outer shell's mould during the carbon-fibre layup process. This integration technique enabled the mirror to become a seamless part of the vehicle's carbon shell, enhancing both aesthetics and aerodynamics.
