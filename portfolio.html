FCC-ee QRL Cryogenic Flow Model

At CERN, I am developing a Python tool that models the helium mass flow and pressure distribution along the QRL (cryogenic transfer line) for the proposed FCC-ee accelerator. The QRL feeds helium to the superconducting cryomodules along the tunnel through six parallel lines operating between 2 K and 55 K. Starting from the far end of the tunnel, the model steps node by node along the line, calculating the mass flow, pressure, and enthalpy at each point while accounting for heat inleaks from the pipe walls and the cryomodule loads at each jumper connection.

Cryomodule Bi-Phase Line Sizing

Another project of mine at CERN is sizing the bi-phase lines for the FCC-ee superconducting radiofrequency cryomodules. The bi-phase line is the horizontal tube above the cavities where saturated liquid helium and its vapour coexist, and it must be large enough to keep the vapour speed low while holding enough liquid to ride through operational transients. I wrote a Python tool that calculates the liquid level profile along the gently sloped tube, the vapour and liquid cross sections, and the time it takes for the line to dry out for a range of pipe diameters.

Maximum Credible Incident (MCI) Analysis

For the FCC-ee superconducting radiofrequency cryomodules at CERN, I analyzed the pressure losses during a Maximum Credible Incident — a sudden loss of insulation vacuum in which air condenses on the cold surfaces and deposits an extreme heat flux into the helium bath. The resulting boil-off must be vented safely through the risers, bi-phase tube, and burst disk line without over-pressurizing the cryomodule. I calculated the venting mass flow following the EN 17527 cryogenic safety standard and compared the pressure drops of four different relief line routing options.

Hydrogen Motorcycle

The project aimed to establish the feasibility of powering a motorcycle with a hydrogen fuel cell. The goals were to source and calculate the total cost of components for the powertrain design and to perform high-level component integration and simulation. My contributions included creating CAD components, designing the fuel cell schematics, and conducting Simulink simulations to assess the performance of the fuel cell, supercapacitor, DC/DC converter, and motor system under various loading conditions.

Arduino Memory Game

The Arduino Joystick Memory Game is an interactive project of mine where players replicate sequences of blinking lights using a joystick. The game setup includes four LEDs arranged in a diamond pattern on a breadboard, with sequences of blinks generated randomly. As players successfully mimic these patterns, the sequences become increasingly complex, adding extra blinks with each level.

Suspension Bushing Test Fixture

At Lucid Motors, one project of mine involved the design of a bushing test fixture. The final design of the fixture was deemed free of proprietary material and can be seen on the right.

The fixture was composed of multiple CNC-machined steel components, which would clamp bushings down and attach them to a CTW LA-48 actuator. The design process was iterative and involved many revisions. FEA was used to enhance the design by reducing material, limiting deflection, and ensuring the parts could handle maximum loading conditions.

The actuator was programmed using chirp signals to dynamically and statically test the bushings. I then created a MATLAB program to analyze data from various suppliers to ensure they met performance benchmarks.

EcoCar Sideview Mirrors

Another EcoCar project of mine involved the design of an aerodynamic side-view mirror. The design included tailored draft angles that could be integrated into the outer shell's mold during the carbon fiber layup process. This integration technique enabled the mirror to become a seamless part of the vehicle's carbon shell, enhancing both aesthetics and aerodynamics.
