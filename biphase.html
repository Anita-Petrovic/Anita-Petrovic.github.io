Cryomodule Bi-Phase Line Sizing

Summary

The bi-phase line runs above the cavities in an FCC-ee cryomodule and carries saturated helium: liquid helium supplies the cavity tanks, while boil-off vapour returns toward the outlet. Together with the level gauge, inlet Joule-Thomson valve, and high-end outlet, it supports stable helium-flow and liquid-level control. Correct sizing is important because the line must keep the returning gas below 5 m/s, provide a measurable operating-level range, and store enough liquid helium to sustain the cryomodule during a 10-15 minute supply interruption.

Background on Cryomodules

The 400 MHz cryomodule is the baseline superconducting RF unit for the FCC-ee Z, W, and ZH operating points. In the current baseline, 66 cryomodules each house four 2-cell 400.79 MHz cavities operating at 4.5 K, for a total of 264 cavities. The bi-phase helium line runs along each cryomodule as the liquid-helium reservoir and supply for the cavity tanks, while supporting stable level measurement and local helium buffering; the complete module sits on the tunnel-floor slope of 0.0025 rad.

Specifications

The bi-phase line specifications were created by Krzysztof Brodzinski.

Specification Requirement
Time to dry If an abrupt valve closure, blockage, or similar incident interrupts the helium supply, the line shall contain sufficient liquid-helium inventory to sustain nominal cryomodule heat loads for 10-15 minutes. In other words, 10-15 minutes should pass before the first cavity starts to dry out and is no longer fully covered by liquid helium.
Level control and measurement The diameter shall account for the level gauge's large uncertainty and limited effective resolution. The expected operational liquid-level variation shall be at least 50 mm, with approximately 100 mm preferred, to provide stable and observable level control.
Gas speed The geometry and helium-level control shall not allow the returning gas to exceed 5 m/s, except during monophasic transients and accidental cases. This limit helps avoid unstable level control, unsteady mass flow, piping vibration, and liquid droplets being carried into the vapour return.
Safety The geometry shall allow gas to exit the helium tanks, including during a maximum credible incident (MCI) and other safety scenarios.
Geometry The design shall account for the tunnel slope. The pumping port shall be located at the higher end of the bi-phase line when the cryomodule is installed on a sloped surface.

Assumptions

  • One end of the bi-phase tube has the highest vapour speed; the highest speed does not occur in the middle of the pipe.
  • The inlet is always at the lower end of the slope, and the outlet is always at the higher end. This arrangement lowers the vapour speed toward the outlet.

How the Code Works

The Python model converts the sloped pipe geometry, heat loads, saturated-helium properties, and operating limits into liquid-height profiles, cross-sectional areas, helium volumes, and dry-out times.

Step 0 - Select the Reference Point

The nominal helium level is set at the halfway mark, so the bi-phase tube is normally half full. Because the cryomodule sits on a 0.0025 rad slope, the calculation needs a fixed reference location to translate that gauge reading into the liquid height elsewhere in the tube.

The centre of the cryomodule is used as the reference point. The pipe wall, rather than the ground, defines the reference height.

D_goal = 0.150                 # pipe diameter [m]
L_cm = 9.0                     # cryomodule length [m]
slope = 0.0025                 # tunnel slope [rad]
reference_point = L_cm / 2
nominal_depth = D_goal / 2     # 50% filling at the gauge

Step 1 - Calculate the Effect of the Slope

To calculate the changing helium volume, the liquid height must be known along the complete pipe. Starting from the reference height, basic trigonometry adds or subtracts the vertical contribution of the 0.0025 rad slope at every axial position.

h(x) = href + tan(α)(xref - x)
def reference_submerged_height(x):
    z_tilt = math.tan(slope) * (given_height_location - x)
    return given_height + z_tilt

Step 2 - Calculate the Area of a Partially Full Pipe

Once the liquid height is known, circular-segment geometry converts it into a liquid cross-sectional area. The formulas are adapted from the partially full pipe-flow reference.

θ = 2 cos-1((R - h) / R)     A = ½R2(θ - sin θ)
def segment_area_from_height(h, R):
    if h <= 0:
        return 0
    if h >= 2 * R:
        return math.pi * R**2

    theta = 2 * math.acos((R - h) / R)
    return 0.5 * R**2 * (theta - math.sin(theta))

Step 3 - Define Heat Loads and Helium Properties

The model defines the 400 MHz cryomodule geometry, temperature, static and dynamic heat loads, RF margin, first-riser location, latent heat, flash fraction, and design margin. Latent heat is the energy needed to change phase without changing temperature: heat added to saturated helium boils liquid into vapour instead of raising its temperature.

case = "4.5K"
L_cm = 9.0                         # m
T = 4.5                            # K
static_heat_load = 32.83           # W per cavity
dyn_heat_load = 128.1              # W per cavity
dry_location = 7.725125            # m
latent_heat = 18839.76740822       # J/kg
flash = 0.033395091
margin = 1.5
cav_cm = 4
Q_cm = margin * (static_heat_load + dyn_heat_load) * cav_cm

The Joule-Thomson valve drops the pressure at approximately constant enthalpy. At the lower pressure, some helium flashes immediately into vapour, so this extra vapour flow must be included in the line sizing.

HEPAK is a helium-property calculation package used at CERN to evaluate thermophysical properties such as saturated liquid and vapour density, enthalpy, and latent heat.

m_dot_l = Q_cm / latent_heat
m_dot_v = flash * m_dot_l
m_dot_cm = m_dot_l + m_dot_v

rho_v = hepak.calculate(3, "T", T, "sV", 0, 2)
rho_l = hepak.calculate(3, "T", T, "sL", 0, 0)

Step 4 - Evaluate the Operating Cases

The code first calculates the vapour area that corresponds to the 5 m/s limit, then maps the liquid profile for the limiting, nominal, and dry cases. It integrates the area along the pipe to obtain the helium inventory and repeats the calculation over a range of diameters.

S_total = math.pi * (D_goal / 2)**2
S_vap = m_dot_cm / (rho_v * speed_v)

positions, heights, areas, volume = compute_profiles_along_pipe(
    D_goal, L_cm, slope, D_goal / 2, L_cm / 2
)

Results

Three cases define the usable operating envelope of the 150 mm line.

Case Importance Visual
5 m/s vapour speed The maximum returning-gas speed is 5 m/s. At the inlet, this limit corresponds to a 140.37 mm liquid height and a 9.63 mm vapour height; this operating point should be avoided because it leaves only 2.71% of the cross-section for vapour.
Nominal operation The line is regulated to 50% filling at the centre gauge. The 0.0025 rad slope produces different liquid heights along the tube, with a calculated liquid volume of approximately 0.0795 m3.
Dry at 7.725 m The first riser begins at 7.725 m and must remain covered. A centre-gauge reading of approximately 8.06 mm corresponds to this dry threshold, with approximately 0.00416 m3 of liquid remaining in the tube.

Time to Dry Outcome

If the helium supply is interrupted, boiling continues while the liquid inventory falls from the nominal level to the first-riser dry threshold. The diameter sweep shows that the current line does not provide the required autonomy.

0.2586 m3 usable liquid-helium volume required for 10 minutes
2.9141 min dry-out time with the current 150 mm diameter
280 mm first tested diameter above 10 minutes (10.4921 min)

For the 150 mm line, only 0.0754 m3 is available between nominal and dry conditions, leaving a 0.1832 m3 shortfall relative to the 10-minute calculation. A 275 mm pipe remains just below the target at 9.9448 minutes, while 280 mm reaches 10.4921 minutes.

How Time to Dry Was Calculated

Step 0 - Integrate the Liquid Area

The code calculates the liquid cross-sectional area at each position and integrates it along the pipe length.

def segment_area_from_height(h, R):
    if h <= 0:
        return 0
    elif h >= 2 * R:
        return math.pi * R**2
    else:
        theta = 2 * math.acos((R - h) / R)
        return 0.5 * R**2 * (theta - math.sin(theta))


z_mid_total = nominal_depth

def local_submerged_height(x):
    z_tilt = math.tan(slope) * (reference_point - x)
    return z_mid_total + z_tilt


def local_area(x):
    h = local_submerged_height(x)
    return segment_area_from_height(h, R)

volume, _ = quad(local_area, 0, L)

Step 1 - Find the Usable Volume

The nominal and dry liquid volumes are calculated, then subtracted to obtain the volume that can boil away before the first riser is exposed.

volume_delta = volume_nominal - volume_dry

Step 2 - Convert Volume to Time

The liquid density from HEPAK converts the usable volume into evaporated mass. Dividing that mass by the boiling mass-flow rate gives the dry-out time.

m_evap = volume_delta * rho_l       # kg
t_seconds = m_evap / m_dot_l        # s
t_minutes = t_seconds / 60

Conclusions

The current 150 mm bi-phase line provides only about 2.9 minutes of autonomy, so it does not meet the 10-15 minute requirement. Based on the CAD inventory of 0.095 m3 for the tube and risers, the design needs an additional 0.1636 m3 of liquid-helium volume to reach the 0.2586 m3 target for 10 minutes. The line therefore needs to be redesigned with a larger diameter or expanded sections while preserving the vapour-speed, level-control, safety, and geometric constraints.
