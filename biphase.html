Cryomodule Bi-Phase Line Sizing

As part of my work in the cryogenics group at CERN, I developed a Python tool to size the bi-phase lines of the FCC-ee superconducting radiofrequency cryomodules. The bi-phase line is the horizontal tube sitting above the cavities in which saturated liquid helium and its vapour coexist: the liquid keeps the cavities immersed at their operating temperature, while the boil-off vapour flows along the tube toward the pumping outlet. Sizing this line correctly matters — the diameter must keep the vapour speed low enough to avoid disturbing the liquid surface, while holding enough helium to ride through operational transients.

How the Sizing Works
  • The cavity heat loads, with an overcapacity margin, are converted into an evaporating mass flow using the latent heat of helium at the operating temperature (2 K for the 800 MHz cryomodules and 4.5 K for the 400 MHz cryomodules)
  • The vapour speed anywhere in the tube is limited to 5 m/s; from the vapour mass flow and density, this sets the minimum cross-sectional area that must remain free for the vapour
  • The tube is installed on a slight slope, so the liquid level — and therefore the split between the liquid and vapour cross sections — changes along its length
  • The code computes the liquid height at each position along the inclined tube and integrates the cross-sectional areas to obtain the total liquid volume for a given filling level
  • Helium properties are evaluated with HEPAK at the saturated conditions of each case
Dry-Out Time and Diameter Selection
  • A key requirement is autonomy: if the liquid supply is interrupted, the line must keep the cavities covered long enough for the machine to respond
  • Starting from the nominal 50% filling level, the code calculates how long it takes for the evaporating flow to lower the liquid level until the first cavity connection is uncovered — the "dry" condition, with the critical location taken from the CAD model
  • This calculation is swept over a range of pipe diameters to find the smallest tube that still meets the required autonomy time, balancing helium inventory against the space the line takes up inside the cryomodule
  • The same tool also answers operational questions, such as how the level reading at the gauge location relates to the liquid volume in the rest of the tube under worst-case vapour flow conditions
